X/Y PAL support

  • Back to the X/Y PAL page
  • Release notes
  • Download on the App Store

X/Y PAL turns your iPhone or iPad into a live touch controller. Move a finger on an XY pad and X/Y PAL sends MIDI Control Change and OSC to your synths, apps and computer at once. This page explains the pads, presets, slot editing and settings of version 2.0, and answers the common questions.

Quick start

  1. Connect your gear. Use a USB MIDI interface, a Bluetooth MIDI device, Network MIDI to a Mac, or OSC over your Wi-Fi.
  2. Add an instrument. Open Settings with the gear button and tap ADD INSTRUMENT. Pick the manufacturer and model from the synth library (or generic MIDI), then the MIDI output and channel. For OSC, add an OSC target first and switch the instrument to OSC.
  3. Pick a preset. Tap the preset name under a pad and choose a factory preset or one of your own. If there is exactly one instrument for the preset’s synth, X/Y PAL binds it for you.
  4. Play. Move your finger on the pad. If a slot light has a red ring, the slot has no instrument yet: tap the hint on the pad to fix it.

The pads

You get two XY pads on an iPhone and four on an iPad in landscape. Each pad has its own preset and its own slot on/off states, and you can play several pads with several fingers at once.

  • Eight slots per pad. X1–X4 follow your finger from left to right, Y1–Y4 from bottom to top. Each slot sends one control to one instrument, scaled to 0–127 within the slot’s range.

  • Slot lights. The column of lights next to a pad switches single slots on or off for that pad. An off slot sends nothing. A red ring means the slot has no instrument yet.

  • Slot lines. The colored lines on the left and bottom edge of the pad show the value every slot is sending.

  • Hints. If a slot has no instrument, or two active slots send the same control to the same place, a hint appears in the top row of the pad. Tap it to go straight to the fix.

  • Preset name. Tap the name under a pad to open the preset list inside the pad: New preset, Duplicate, the factory presets (Minilogue Mood, Grandtriarch, FM fun) and your own presets, each with a delete button. The pad sends nothing while the list is open.

  • Pencil. Opens the edit view for the pad’s preset.

A touch sends its position at once, without waiting for you to move. Sending never blocks the screen: a slow or missing output cannot stall the pads or your other outputs.

Editing a preset

Tap the pencil under a pad. At the top you see the eight slot lights in two trays, one for X and one for Y. Tap a light to pick the slot you want to edit, or double-tap it to switch the slot on or off for this pad. The edit view has four pages:

  • INSTRUMENT · CC – choose the instrument the slot plays through, then the control. With the synth library you pick a named control such as CUTOFF instead of a CC number. You can also create a new instrument right here.

  • RANGE – set MIN and MAX (0–127). The test strip sends a value to the slot’s instrument, so you can hear the range while you set it.

  • RELEASE – ACTIVE ON THIS PAD, INVERSE (the slot runs from MAX to MIN) and BACK TO MIN ON RELEASE (the slot jumps back when you lift your finger). With INVERSE and BACK TO MIN together, release returns to the top of the range.

  • LOOK – the slot’s name and color. They show on the light, the slot line and the display header.

Tap the preset name to rename it, and SAVE to keep your changes. Editing a factory preset makes a copy ("… copy"), so the original always stays. If you save without changes, the copy is dropped again.

An orange ring on slot lights warns you that two active slots send the same control to the same place. It does not block you.

Settings

Open Settings with the gear button. Changes apply at once, without a restart. Settings have three pages; switch between them with the arrows at the bottom:

  • INSTRUMENTS – your gear. Each instrument has a name, a synth model and a kind: MIDI (output and channel 1–16) or OSC (target and address prefix). You can rename an instrument, change its synth, switch it between MIDI and OSC, or delete it.

  • MIDI OUTPUTS – all MIDI outputs of your device. Pick the default output, and see which instruments play on each output. An output that is gone stays in the list, marked OFFLINE, as long as an instrument still points at it.

  • OSC TARGETS – add, edit (name, host, port) and delete OSC targets. Each target shows its instruments.

The buttons at the top add an instrument, pair a Bluetooth MIDI device and add an OSC target. Below the pages:

  • HAPTIC FEEDBACK – a small tick under your finger on iPhone. It is on by default. On an iPad it shows NOT ON THIS DEVICE.

  • NETWORK MIDI – switch it on and your iPhone or iPad joins the network MIDI session, so a Mac on the same network can connect in Audio MIDI Setup. Then choose the network session as the MIDI output of an instrument.

Presets and instruments

A preset never names a device or a channel. It names synth parts, and each device binds those parts to its own instruments. That’s why a preset works on any iPhone or iPad, with any gear, once its parts are bound.

OSC format

X/Y PAL sends one OSC message per value change:

<prefix>/cc/<n> <value>
  • <n> is the CC number of the slot, <value> an int32 of 0–127. Example: /xypal/cc/43 64
  • The default prefix is /xypal. Set your own per instrument. An empty prefix sends /cc/43
  • iOS asks once for local network access when the first OSC message goes to your network. Allow it, or OSC cannot reach other computers. You can change it later in the iOS Settings under Privacy & Security > Local Network

To check what arrives, try the free MIDI / OSC Monitor Max patch.

FAQ

Does the app make sounds?

No. X/Y PAL is a controller. It sends MIDI CC and OSC to your synths, apps or DAW, so you need something that makes the sound.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 16 or later, in every orientation.

Do I lose my presets when I update to 2.0?

No. Presets and settings from version 1 are migrated when you open 2.0, and every route is kept: slots that sent to the same device and channel become one part, and each device and channel becomes an instrument.

Why does a slot light have a red ring?

The slot belongs to a part that is not bound to an instrument on this device yet. Tap the hint on the pad ("… not set up · tap to fix") and pick or create an instrument.

Why is my MIDI output marked OFFLINE?

The output was there before but is not connected now, for example a USB interface that is unplugged or a Bluetooth device that is off. Connect it again, or point the instrument at another output.

How can I control Ableton Live with X/Y PAL?

  1. Connect. With a USB cable, open Audio MIDI Setup on the Mac and click “Enable” under your iPhone or iPad. Without a cable, switch on NETWORK MIDI in X/Y PAL and connect to the session in Audio MIDI Setup > MIDI Network Setup.
  2. Enable Remote. In Ableton Live, open Settings > Link, Tempo & MIDI and switch on “Remote” for the input of your iOS device.
  3. Add an instrument. In X/Y PAL, add an instrument whose MIDI output is “IDAM MIDI Host” (USB) or the network session.
  4. Map. Bind your preset to that instrument, press MIDI map mode in Live, click a parameter and move the slot.

Does X/Y PAL collect any data?

No. Everything is stored on your device. X/Y PAL has no accounts and no tracking, and it opens no audio device.

Where is the guide in the app?

Tap the info button next to the gear. It has a short guide (PLAY, EDIT, SETUP), the app version, a Help & How-To button that opens this site, and a button to rate the app.

How to report a bug

Write to hey [at] audio [dot] ooo and include:

  1. The X/Y PAL version (on the info screen) and your iOS version
  2. Your device model
  3. How you connect: USB, Bluetooth MIDI, Network MIDI or OSC, and which synth, app or DAW receives it
  4. What you did, what you expected and what happened instead
  5. The instrument, MIDI channel and CC number, or the OSC target and prefix, of the slot involved
